# SOMAD Agro-Oil Processing Limited

Standard Operating Procedures · Staff operating manual · SOP 01–06

**Revision:** 16 November 2026. Revised for Director-led procurement, Storekeeper-led delivery inspection and acceptance, assigned Operator handling duties, and the current app workflow.

**Roles:** Director = the company’s decision-maker, using the account with director-level permissions. Mill Manager = Manager account. Storekeeper = Storekeeper account. Operators are responsible for lifting, unloading, loading and moving materials; they do not submit a second set of weights.

## How work is recorded in the app

### One source of measured quantities

The Storekeeper is responsible for all weighing and measurement: deliveries, kernel issues, finished-product receipts, dispatches, sample tests and verified physical returns or losses. Operators are responsible for lifting, unloading, loading and moving materials, including placing bags on the scale and removing them. The Storekeeper supervises the weighing, checks the readings and records every measurement. The Manager plans, supervises and authorises work; the Manager does not enter a separate set of weights or repeat the Storekeeper’s entries.

### Authorise, weigh and save

- **Purchases:** Director, or Manager acting within delegated authority, selects the date, supplier, material and approved unit cost, then chooses Authorise Purchase. When a kernel delivery enters the factory, the Storekeeper takes responsibility for receiving, inspection, weighing and sampling. She accepts it herself if all checks and approved terms are satisfactory. If there is a problem, she holds the delivery and gives the Director her findings and reasons for withholding acceptance. The Director decides whether to reject it, approve acceptance or order price renegotiation. Only after acceptance and confirmation of any revised authorised terms does the Storekeeper open Pending Weighing, record the actual accepted quantity and select Confirm Receipt. The app creates the purchase record, its total and its inventory receipt together.
- **Production:** Manager or Director authorises one production day, including its date, operating hours and downtime. Under Pending Weighing the Storekeeper uses Issue Kernel for every kernel issue and Receive Product for each separate receipt of PKO, PKC or sludge. Entries accumulate into one production record for that date and update stock as saved.
- **Sales:** Director sets approved product prices on Prices. Manager or Director selects the date, customer and product and chooses Authorise Sale; the app uses the applicable saved price. The Storekeeper records the actual dispatched quantity and chooses Confirm Dispatch. The app creates the sale, total, invoice identity and stock issue together.
- **Quality:** A kernel sample test can be entered during the first purchase receipt or added later under Data → Purchases Data → Kernel quality · Sample tests. Use grams for both sample and impurity weights. Quality percentages do not automatically reduce stock or change the supplier’s payment.
- **Corrections:** Refer incorrect saved quantities to the Director. The Director uses the relevant correction controls based on the Storekeeper’s verified measurements. Do not cancel an authorisation or add an unrelated inventory entry to undo an already saved purchase, sale or production entry.

### Cancellations, closing and reopening

A Manager can cancel only an open authorisation they created, before quantities have been saved. A Manager cannot cancel a Director-created authorisation or another user’s authorisation. The Director can cancel any eligible open authorisation before saved quantities exist. Cancellation does not reverse saved stock.

Once a production issue or receipt has been saved, retain the daily record and use the appropriate Director correction controls for mistakes. The Storekeeper selects Finish Day after recording that date’s actual activities. Only the Director can reopen a finished production day; reopening preserves the existing entries. Do not create a second production authorisation for the same recorded date.

### Records without a dedicated app screen

The app’s manual Inventory Report form and manual stock corrections are Director controls. The Storekeeper still owns the measurement: keep source logs for meter readings, consumable issues/returns, bag counts, maintenance, incidents, inspection exceptions and waste disposal where no dedicated Storekeeper screen exists. Submit verified quantities to the Director for any necessary approved manual inventory entry. Items not configured in the app remain in the approved supporting log until Director configures them.

Use the approved supporting log for procurement delegation, supplier-payment approvals, disposal authorisations and other tasks without an app screen. Keep the supporting approval in the company’s approved record. Supplier-payment approval, quality acceptance exceptions and permission to release goods remain management responsibilities; a saved app transaction does not itself prove that these approvals were given.

### Review and reconciliation

Review saved purchases, production entries, sales and inventory movements from Data. The app links physical movements to their source record. Do not enter the same receipt or dispatch again in Inventory. The Director reviews Checks and the dashboard, investigates discrepancies and corrects the linked source. Dashboard costing and profit may be estimated while production receipts, expenses or other required records remain incomplete; do not force weights to make a report agree.

## SOP 01 · Raw Materials and Consumables Procurement and Receiving

**SOP number:** SOMAD-SOP-001. **Palm kernel procurement:** Director; the Director may delegate specified procurement tasks to the Mill Manager. **Physical receiving and measurements:** Storekeeper. **Lifting, unloading and loading:** Production Operators.

### 1. Purpose and scope

Buy good-quality kernels and suitable consumables at approved prices; avoid shortages, inflated costs, unverified deliveries and duplicate stock records. This SOP covers palm kernels, lubricants, grease, machine oil, diesel where applicable, spare parts, belts, bearings, filter cloths, packaging, cleaning materials and other factory supplies. Electricity is also covered in SOP 03.

### 2. Staff responsibilities

#### 2.1 Director

The Director is responsible for palm kernel procurement: finding and selecting suppliers, comparing quality and prices, negotiating purchase terms, agreeing delivery arrangements and controlling or authorising supplier payments. The Director may delegate supplier search, negotiations, ordering and delivery arrangements to the Manager within stated limits, while retaining procurement oversight, payment approval and approval of exceptions. Keep the scope of delegation and approved commercial terms in the company’s approved record; the app has no separate procurement-delegation workflow.

#### 2.2 Mill Manager

Monitor production requirements, coordinate delivery arrangements, assign Operators for lifting and loading, and pass the approved purchase documents to the Storekeeper. Once a kernel delivery enters the factory, the Storekeeper takes responsibility for all receiving and inspection steps; the sourcing Manager must not approve acceptance, choose the inspection result or override her findings. Procure palm kernels only when the Director delegates that responsibility; do not independently commit the company outside the delegation. Arrange other supplies within the Director’s approval. Prepare app authorisations only within the approved commercial terms. The Manager does not enter received weights or submit a second purchase-quantity report.

#### 2.3 Storekeeper

Once a kernel delivery enters the factory, the Storekeeper takes responsibility for receiving it. She checks the supplier and material, supervises weighing, selects representative samples, conducts the quality inspection and impurity tests, and records the findings. She accepts the delivery when every required check and the approved terms are satisfactory. If she is not satisfied, she holds the delivery, contacts the Director directly and explains the problem and her reasons for not accepting it. The Director decides whether to reject the delivery, approve acceptance or order price renegotiation. The Storekeeper does not negotiate prices, authorise procurement or approve supplier payments; her acceptance of a satisfactory delivery is a receiving decision. She remains independent of pressure from the sourcing Manager or supplier.

#### 2.4 Production Operators

Operators are responsible for lifting, unloading, loading and moving materials. They place bags on the scale, remove them after the Storekeeper records the weight, carry samples and sort material when instructed by the Storekeeper, and move accepted deliveries into storage. These are assigned duties, not optional assistance. During receiving they follow the Storekeeper’s instructions for handling the delivery. They do not select the acceptance decision or submit a competing set of weights.

### 3. Procurement and delivery arrangements

1. The Storekeeper reports available kernel and consumable stock. The Manager advises production requirements early enough to prevent stoppages.
2. The Director arranges palm kernel procurement or gives the Manager a clear delegation, including any price, supplier or spending limits.
3. The Director or delegated Manager compares supplier offers, quality, availability, transport, loading and offloading terms. Keep supplier details, expected delivery, proposed quantity and agreed costs in the purchasing record.
4. The Director approves commercial terms and controls or authorises payment. The delegated Manager must refer changes outside the agreed limits back to the Director.
5. Director or the authorised Manager enters the date, supplier, material and approved unit cost in the Purchase form, then selects Authorise Purchase. This creates a Pending Weighing task; it is not proof of delivery and does not add stock.
6. Inform the Storekeeper of the expected delivery. Keep the supplier’s invoice, receipt and delivery documents. The app does not require the Manager to enter the final delivery weight.

### 4. Palm kernel receiving and inspection

#### 4.1 Check the delivery

As soon as kernels enter the factory, the Storekeeper takes responsibility for the delivery and all receiving steps. She checks the approved purchase details, supplier, material and bags presented; organises weighing and sampling; and inspects for wet bags, mould, water damage, stones, excessive shells and other concerns. Operators carry out the lifting, unloading, loading and positioning of bags under her receiving instructions. The Manager who sourced the kernels provides purchase information and handles supplier communication when instructed, but does not decide acceptance or override the inspection.

#### 4.2 Weigh the kernels

1. Weigh every bag on the approved factory scale. The Storekeeper supervises and records the actual weight; Operators handle the bags safely.
2. Deduct bag weight only if the Director has instructed that it be deducted for that supply. If instructed, use the approved bag-weight method; otherwise do not automatically subtract bag weight. Record the Director’s instruction and whether the receiving figure includes or excludes bags. Apply any separate container-weight instruction as agreed by the Director, then total the measured quantities on that approved basis.
3. The supplier may observe, but the supplier’s invoice weight does not replace the Storekeeper’s measurement.
4. Use the Storekeeper’s verified factory quantity on the Director-approved bag-weight basis and the approved unit cost. Do not substitute the proposed order quantity. If the bag-weight instruction is unclear, ask the Director before confirming the receipt.

#### 4.3 Take a representative small sample

1. Select at least 10 bags or 10% of delivered bags, whichever is greater, up to 30 bags. If fewer than 10 bags arrive, sample all available bags.
2. Take small portions from different parts of the selected bags, combine approximately 10 kg and mix thoroughly.
3. Take approximately 2 kg of the mixed material for the test. Record its actual weight, not an assumed 2 kg.
4. The Storekeeper selects the representative sample and directs sorting of clean kernels from shells, stones, dirt and foreign material. Operators carry out the required lifting, sample handling and sorting under her instructions. The Storekeeper weighs the full sample before sorting and the separated impurities. The sourcing Manager or supplier must not choose the sample or determine its reported result.
5. Enter both test weights in grams. A 2 kg sample is 2,000 g; 0.16 kg impurities is 160 g.

#### 4.4 Save the sample test

In Pending Weighing, tick Record a sample test with this receipt, enter Total sample weight (g) and Impurity weight (g), and review the preview. Do not select Confirm Receipt while the delivery is on hold or awaiting the Director’s decision. After acceptance and confirmation of the approved price and weighing basis, Confirm Receipt saves the sample, purchase and stock receipt together. The control is optional in the app; required company quality checks must still be carried out before acceptance.

Additional tests are entered under Data → Purchases Data → the kernel purchase → Kernel quality · Sample tests. The app saves each test and calculates the combined result from the total impurity weight divided by the total sample weight. It does not take a simple average of different-sized samples.

**Formula:** Impurity % = impurity weight ÷ sample weight × 100. Clean kernel % = 100 − impurity %.

**Example:** 160 g impurities in a 2,000 g sample gives 8% impurity. Two samples of 1,000 g/40 g impurities and 500 g/50 g impurities give a combined 90 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = 6% impurity.

A sample must be positive; impurities may be zero but cannot exceed the sample weight. The Director can delete an incorrect sample and record its replacement. A purchase with sample tests cannot be changed to another material until Director removes those tests. No test is different from a recorded 0% test.

#### 4.5 Apply the quality acceptance rule

The existing company acceptance limit remains 10% impurities, with a satisfactory visual inspection. Look for wet kernels, damp bags, mould and water damage; a moisture meter is not required under this SOP at present.

The Storekeeper must not accept kernels above 10% impurities, or kernels with serious contamination, mould or water damage, on her own authority. She puts the delivery on hold, keeps it separate from accepted stock and contacts the Director with the findings, test result and reasons for withholding acceptance. The Director decides whether to reject it, approve an exception or order price renegotiation. The Storekeeper records the decision and follows the Director’s instructions. The app calculates percentages; it does not automatically approve, reject or renegotiate a delivery.

Do not hide a failed test. Do not automatically deduct a sample’s estimated impurity percentage from the delivery weight, purchase total or supplier payment. Any agreed commercial adjustment requires the Director’s decision and the appropriate verified correction.

#### 4.6 Accept or reject the delivery

1. The Storekeeper takes over receiving responsibility when the kernels enter the factory and completes all weighing, sampling, inspection and document checks.
2. If the delivery passes the required checks, is within the 10% impurity limit and matches the approved terms, she accepts it without waiting for the Director to attend the site. She records only the actual accepted quantity on the instructed bag-weight basis and completes Confirm Receipt.
3. If any check is unsatisfactory, or she has a reasonable concern about quality, quantity, documents, price or the weighing basis, she does not accept or confirm the receipt. She keeps the delivery separate and immediately contacts the Director directly.
4. She gives the Director the supplier and purchase details, measured weights, sample and impurity weights, impurity percentage, visual findings, discrepancies, and a clear explanation of why she does not want to accept the delivery. Send photographs or other supporting evidence where practical, and keep the findings in the receiving log.
5. The Director reviews the information remotely and decides whether to reject the delivery, accept it as an authorised exception, request further checks or order price renegotiation. Neither the sourcing Manager nor the supplier may overrule the Storekeeper while that decision is pending.
6. If the Director orders price renegotiation, the Director negotiates or instructs the Manager to negotiate. The Storekeeper keeps the delivery on hold until the Director confirms the revised terms. Ensure the Pending Weighing authorisation shows the approved unit cost before Confirm Receipt. If replacement authorisation is needed, the Director or an eligible originating Manager cancels the still-uncompleted authorisation and issues the corrected one under the cancellation rules in the workflow guide.
7. The Storekeeper records the Director’s decision and any revised terms before completing an approved receipt. Rejected material is not added to usable stock. Operators are responsible for the lifting, loading and movement of rejected material as instructed, and for moving accepted kernels into storage.
8. Physical entry into the factory, unloading or a purchase authorisation does not itself mean a delivery has been accepted. Do not use Confirm Receipt merely to log the arrival of a delivery that is still being inspected or held.

### 5. Consumables and other supplies

Check the material, specification, condition and actual accepted quantity. Measure diesel in litres, electricity in kWh and other app items in their configured units; keep supporting records for supplies not configured in the app. Report incorrect, damaged or missing items before acceptance. Store diesel in suitable containers and follow SOP 03 and SOP 05.

The Storekeeper completes the authorised purchase in Pending Weighing using Confirm Receipt. The purchase and linked stock receipt are generated once. Do not re-enter the same received quantity as a manual inventory receipt.

### 6. Purchase, inventory and supporting records

Director or delegated Manager authorises the supplier, material, date and unit cost. The Storekeeper enters the measured quantity, vehicle number and scale ticket where available. The app calculates the purchase total and records inventory from this single receipt.

Keep bag-by-bag weights, delivery documents and quality-approval notes in the receiving log where the app has no field. Enter separately charged operating costs in Expenses when applicable; do not record a cost twice.

Once a week, the Storekeeper counts stored bags against the receiving and issue logs. Keep delivery bags together where practical. Intact bags do not need reweighing solely for the count, but opened or partly used bags must be weighed when their remaining contents are returned. Do not assume equal bag weights. Report tampering, damage or differences to the Director, who decides whether to reweigh selected bags. Bag counts check numbers of bags, not exact stock weight.

If a saved receipt is wrong, refer it to the Director for a linked purchase correction. Do not cancel a completed authorisation or create an unrelated stock entry to conceal the mistake. Deleting a purchase also removes its linked quality tests; review the record before an authorised deletion.

### 7. Mandatory rules and non-compliance

The Director leads palm kernel procurement and may delegate sourcing to the Manager. Once the delivery enters the factory, the Storekeeper is responsible for receiving and inspection, and accepts satisfactory deliveries herself. She holds any unsatisfactory delivery and reports the findings and her reasons directly to the Director, who decides rejection, exceptional acceptance or price renegotiation. The sourcing Manager cannot override her inspection. All physical quantities come from the Storekeeper. Operators are responsible for lifting, unloading and loading. Every kernel bag must be weighed; deduct bag weight only on the Director’s instruction. Apply the 10% quality limit and obtain the Director’s decision for exceptions. Use one purchase receipt and its linked stock movement. Do not falsify weights, test results, prices, supplier arrangements or stock records. Unauthorised procurement, concealed quality failures and dishonest records may result in disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

## SOP 02 · Production Operations and Quality Control

**SOP number:** SOMAD-SOP-002. **Operations:** Mill Manager. **All weighing and physical entries:** Storekeeper. **Machine operation and handling:** Production Operators. **Review and corrections:** Director.

### 1. Purpose and scope

Process palm kernels into good-quality PKO and PKC, separately record recovered sludge, minimise waste, operate safely and keep accurate linked production and stock records. This covers kernel preparation, crushing, roasting, expelling, settling, filtering, storage, power failures and daily closing.

### 2. Responsibilities

The Manager plans and authorises the production day, assigns Operators, checks machine readiness, supervises settings and quality, records operating hours and downtime, and reports problems. The Manager reviews the Storekeeper’s saved quantities and does not enter another set of production weights.

The Storekeeper weighs and records each kernel issue and each receipt of PKO, PKC or sludge. Operators move materials, operate assigned machinery, collect products, carry out the lifting and handling required for weighing, and clean safely. The Director approves major changes and repairs, reviews discrepancies, and uses Director correction or reopening controls where required.

### 3. App authorisation and continuous recording

1. Manager or Director selects the production date, operating hours and downtime and authorises one production day. Do not enter kernel or product weights in the Manager’s authorisation.
2. In Pending Weighing the Storekeeper selects Issue Kernel, weighs the actual kernels issued and saves the entry. Stock reduces and the day’s production record is created or updated.
3. Whenever a finished product is ready, the Storekeeper selects Receive Product, chooses PKO, PKC or Sludge and records that product’s actual quantity. Save one selected product per receipt.
4. Continue issuing kernels and receiving products as work progresses. Do not wait for all oil from one kernel issue before saving the next issue, and do not try to match each output weighing to one input batch.
5. Every saved activity updates the same daily production totals and its linked stock movements. Do not submit a second inventory form or a separate Manager quantity report.
6. Keep scale tickets and supporting physical logs where applicable. Review individual issues and receipts in the production entry history and Data pages.

### 4. Production procedure

#### 4.1 Plan the work

The Manager checks power availability, machine safety, staffing and available stock; explains the work and requests kernels from the Storekeeper. An intended production target is a plan, not a measured quantity to be entered as used. Do not start an unsafe or seriously faulty machine.

#### 4.2 Collect kernels

Operators bring the requested bags to the weighing area. The Storekeeper checks available stock, weighs the actual quantity and saves Issue Kernel against the open authorisation before material enters production. The Manager coordinates the work but does not re-enter the weight. Additional kernels follow the same process; no Operator takes stock without the Storekeeper recording it.

#### 4.3 Preparing Kernels for Crushing

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Production Operators

1. The Mill Manager tells the Operators which kernels to prepare.

2. The Operators open the bags and load the kernels into the crusher hopper.

3. The Operators look out for stones, metal pieces, large shells and other unwanted materials.

4. The Operators remove unwanted materials that can be safely removed before crushing.

5. The Mill Manager checks that kernels are entering the crusher at a steady rate.

##### Rules

- Do not pour kernels onto dirty floors.

- Do not put hands inside the crusher while it is running.

- Do not overload the crusher.

- If the crusher becomes blocked, switch it off and safely isolate it before clearing the blockage.

#### 4.4 Crushing the Kernels

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

Required crushed kernel size: To be confirmed

Crusher feeding rate: To be confirmed

##### Procedure

1. The Operator checks the crusher for blockage, damage or loose parts.

2. The Mill Manager confirms that the crusher is ready.

3. The Operator starts the crusher.

4. The Operators feed kernels into the crusher steadily.

5. The assigned Operator watches the crushed kernels coming out.

6. The Mill Manager checks the size of the crushed kernels regularly.

7. If the pieces are too big or too fine, the Mill Manager instructs the Operator to stop the machine.

8. The machine must be safely isolated before any adjustment.

9. The Mill Manager checks the crushed kernels again after adjustment.

##### Quality check

The crushed kernels must be of a suitable size for roasting and oil extraction.

The correct size will be decided during machine testing and added to this SOP.

Operators must not change the crusher settings without the Mill Manager's permission.

#### 4.5 Moving Kernels with the Conveyors

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

1. The Operator checks that the conveyor is clear and ready.

2. The Mill Manager confirms that the receiving hopper or machine is ready.

3. The Operator starts the conveyor.

4. The Operators watch the movement of kernels.

5. If the receiving hopper is becoming full, the Operator stops or reduces feeding.

6. If the conveyor becomes blocked, the Operator stops it and informs the Mill Manager.

7. The conveyor must be safely isolated before anyone attempts to remove a blockage.

#### 4.6 Loading the Roaster

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Assigned Operator Supporting: Other Operators when needed

Roaster rated capacity: 170 kg per batch

Approved quantity per batch: To be confirmed Approved loading time: To be confirmed

##### Procedure

1. The Mill Manager confirms that the roaster is ready and the previous batch has been removed.

2. The Operator checks that the feeding hopper is clear.

3. The Mill Manager tells the Operator the quantity to load.

4. The Operators load the crushed kernels using the approved feeding method.

5. The assigned Operator watches the loading to prevent spillage or overfilling.

6. The Mill Manager confirms that the correct quantity has been loaded.

7. The Storekeeper is responsible for any batch weight used in records. The Manager supervises the loading plan and does not enter a competing measured quantity.

##### Rules

- Do not load more than the approved quantity.

- Do not put hands or tools inside the rotating drum.

- Do not allow the feeding hopper to touch moving parts.

- Report unusual noise, vibration or spillage immediately.

#### 4.7 Roasting the Kernels

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

Approved temperature: To be confirmed Approved roasting time: To be confirmed

Drum rotation setting: To be confirmed

##### Procedure

1. The Mill Manager confirms the roasting temperature and time.

2. The Operator starts the roaster using the approved settings.

3. The Operator watches the temperature and roasting time.

4. The Mill Manager checks the condition of the kernels during roasting.

5. If the temperature becomes too high or kernels begin to burn, the Operator immediately informs the Mill Manager.

6. The Mill Manager instructs the Operator on how to correct the problem.

7. When roasting is complete, the Mill Manager confirms that the kernels are ready.

8. The Operator discharges the roasted kernels into the receiving hopper.

9. The Mill Manager checks that the kernels are properly roasted and not burnt before sending them to the expeller.

##### Quality check

Kernels should be roasted evenly without burning.

The correct temperature and roasting time will be decided during production testing.

Once these settings are approved, Operators must follow them and must not change them without permission.

#### 4.8 Feeding the Expeller and Extracting Oil

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

Approved feeding rate: To be confirmed

##### Procedure

1. The Operator checks the expeller for blockage, damage or loose parts.

2. The Mill Manager confirms that it is ready.

3. The Operator starts the expeller following the approved startup method.

4. The Operators transfer roasted kernels into the expeller feeding hopper.

5. The assigned Operator makes sure kernels enter the expeller steadily.

6. The Mill Manager watches the oil coming out and the PKC being discharged.

7. If the machine becomes overloaded, makes unusual noise or stops working properly, the Operator stops or reduces feeding and informs the Mill Manager.

8. The Mill Manager checks the problem before allowing production to continue.

9. Oil coming from the expeller is directed to the settling tank.

10. PKC coming from the expeller is collected in the designated area.

The Mill Manager must watch for:

- Unusually low oil output.

- Too much oil remaining in the PKC.

- Frequent blockages.

- Unusual noise or overheating.

- Poor or uneven feeding.

Rule: Never put hands or tools inside the expeller while its moving parts can operate.

#### 4.9 Collecting and Storing PKC

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Production Operators Stock Verification: Storekeeper

1. The Operators collect PKC coming out of the expeller.

2. The Mill Manager makes sure it does not mix with dirt, water or other unwanted materials.

3. The Operators allow hot PKC to cool safely before bagging or storage where necessary.

4. The Operators move the PKC to the weighing area.

5. The Storekeeper weighs the PKC and saves Receive Product → PKC under the daily authorisation.

6. The Manager reviews the saved PKC receipt and checks product quality; no separate Manager weight is entered.

7. The Operators move the PKC into storage.

8. The saved PKC receipt updates inventory automatically; do not enter it again in Inventory.

Do not pack or pile very hot PKC in a way that could cause overheating or fire.

#### 4.10 Oil Collection and Settling

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

Approved settling time: To be confirmed Maximum settling tank level: To be confirmed

##### Procedure

1. The Operator checks that the settling tank is clean and ready.

2. The Mill Manager confirms that the oil pipes are properly connected.

3. The Operator watches the crude oil entering the settling tank.

4. The Operator makes sure the tank does not overflow.

5. The Mill Manager ensures that the oil stays in the settling tank for the approved time so that heavier dirt and particles can settle.

6. The Operator transfers the settled oil to the filter press without disturbing the sludge at the bottom unnecessarily.

7. The Mill Manager checks when sludge needs to be removed and instructs the Operators to clean the tank when required.

The correct settling time will be decided during production testing.

#### 4.11 Filtering the Oil

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

Supporting: Other Operators when needed

Approved filter pressure: To be confirmed Maximum safe pressure: To be confirmed from machine specifications Filter cloth cleaning interval: To be confirmed

##### Before filtering

1. The Operator checks the filter press, pipes, hoses and filter cloths.

2. The Mill Manager confirms that the filter plates and cloths are properly fitted.

3. The Operator checks that the filtered oil outlet leads to the correct tank.

##### During filtering

1. The Mill Manager instructs the Operator to start filtering.

2. The Operator starts the oil pump.

3. The Operator watches the pressure, oil flow and any leakage.

4. The Mill Manager checks the filtered oil for visible dirt.

5. If oil flow becomes too slow, pressure becomes too high or the filtered oil is dirty, the Operator stops filtering and informs the Mill Manager.

6. The Mill Manager decides whether the filter press needs cleaning or repair.

##### Cleaning the filter cloths

1. The Operator switches off the pump.

2. The Mill Manager makes sure the pump is safely isolated and all pressure inside the filter press has been released.

3. The Operators open the filter press using the approved method.

4. The Operators remove the dirt and filter cake collected inside.

5. The Operators clean the filter cloths.

6. The Mill Manager checks the cloths for tears, blockage or damage.

7. Any damaged cloth must be replaced.

8. The Operators close and secure the filter press.

9. The Mill Manager checks that it is properly closed before allowing filtering to continue.

##### When should filter cloths be cleaned?

During the first production trials, the Mill Manager will check how many filtering cycles can be completed before the cloths become blocked or the oil flow becomes too slow.

A normal cleaning interval will then be agreed upon.

However, the cloths must be cleaned earlier if necessary.

Rule: Never open the filter press while it is under pressure.

#### 4.12 Transferring Finished PKO into Storage

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Assigned Operator Stock Verification: Storekeeper

1. The Mill Manager confirms that the final PKO storage tank is ready.

2. The Operator checks the pump, pipes and valves.

3. The Operator transfers the filtered oil into the final storage tank.

4. The Mill Manager watches the transfer to prevent overflow, leakage or mixing with unfiltered oil.

5. The Storekeeper measures the finished PKO and saves Receive Product → PKO under the daily authorisation.

6. The Manager reviews the saved receipt and product quality; no separate Manager weight is entered.

7. The saved PKO receipt updates inventory automatically. Do not submit another receipt for the same oil.

Important: Only filtered oil transferred into the final storage tank is counted as PKO produced.

Oil still in the settling tank or filter press must not be counted as finished PKO.

#### 4.13 Recording, returns and yield review

Save actual issues and receipts throughout the day, not only when a whole production run is complete. The Storekeeper’s saved entries provide the production totals and inventory movements. The Manager reviews hours, downtime and operational concerns; Director investigates incorrect entries using the relevant correction controls.

If unused kernels that never entered processing are physically returned, the Storekeeper weighs them and keeps a return record. The app has no dedicated Storekeeper kernel-return button. Ask Director to correct or reverse the appropriate original kernel issue using the verified return, including reopening that day if required. Do not receive unused kernels as PKO, PKC or sludge, or create a duplicate purchase. Kernels inside machines are not available raw-material stock and must not be recorded as returned.

For operational yield review, use actual net kernel issues and weighed finished products. PKO yield % = finished PKO ÷ verified kernels used × 100; PKC yield % is calculated in the same way. Do not deduct the estimated sample impurity percentage from the official kernel quantity. For example, 360 kg of oil from 1,000 kg of kernels gives 36%, regardless of a 10% delivery-sample impurity result.

Daily figures can include outputs from earlier issues and inputs whose products have not yet been received. Treat those daily yields and costing estimates as provisional; they are not proof of a fully matched processing run. Review actual weighed removals, unfinished material and missing receipts with the Director rather than changing weights to force agreement.

#### 4.14 Checking the Quality of PKO and PKC

Responsible: Mill Manager Supporting: Production Operators

##### PKO quality checks

The Mill Manager must check for:

- Visible dirt or particles in the oil.

- Water contamination.

- Unusual smell.

- Poor filtering.

##### PKC quality checks

The Mill Manager must check for:

- Too much oil remaining in the cake.

- Dirt or other contamination.

- Unusual smell or appearance.

- Excessive heat or moisture during storage.

##### Production checks

The Mill Manager must monitor:

- Quantity of oil produced.

- Quantity of PKC produced.

- Oil yield.

- Machine stoppages.

- Losses and waste.

The required product quality standards will be agreed upon after production testing.

#### 4.15 Power failure and unfinished production

1. Operators stop feeding kernels and safely switch off and secure machines against unexpected restart. Follow SOP 05 for isolation, pressure, heat and stored energy.
2. The Manager identifies unfinished material, stops the roaster heat source safely, records the interruption and ensures hot equipment is not left in a dangerous condition. Operators secure the work area and materials.
3. The Storekeeper saves the actual kernel issues and finished-product receipts already measured. Do not withhold those records merely because processing is unfinished.
4. Do not guess the weight of kernels trapped in machines, count unfiltered oil as finished PKO or return unfinished material to available kernel stock.
5. If no further physical entries will be made for that date, the Storekeeper may Finish Day after at least one saved entry. This closes entry access; it does not declare that every issued kernel has physically finished processing or empty the machines.
6. When work resumes, the Manager confirms power and machine safety. Operators resume safely with the unfinished material. The Storekeeper records only newly issued kernels as new issues and records products on the date they are actually weighed and received, under that date’s authorisation.
7. Do not issue the same kernels twice, postpone all new-day records until an old batch is matched, or backdate later physical output simply to force earlier yields to balance.
8. If a measurement genuinely belonged to a finished date and was omitted or wrong, refer it to the Director. Only the Director reopens the original date for the authorised correction; do not create a second daily production record.

Unfinished material is used internally in costing. It is not a separate available-stock balance or a new Storekeeper control. Costs and profit can remain estimated while receipts are incomplete. Keep operational interruption notes in the approved supporting log.

#### 4.16 Closing the production day

The Manager stops new feeding and supervises completion or safe securing of material, shutdown and cleaning. Operators isolate equipment as required and clean the work area. The Storekeeper saves all actual issues and receipts made that day, reports unused returns and discrepancies, then selects Finish Day when the day’s recording is complete. The Manager does not submit a second quantity report. Only the Director can reopen the finished day for additional entries or corrections.

## SOP 03 · Energy and Consumables Usage Control

**SOP number:** SOMAD-SOP-003. **Operations:** Mill Manager. **Physical measurements and source logs:** Storekeeper. **Assigned physical work:** Production Operators. **Approval and review:** Director.

### 1. Purpose and scope

Prevent unnecessary waste, keep electricity and consumables available, and record purchases and use accurately. NEPA electricity is the primary source. The company does not currently have a generator; diesel/generator operating instructions apply only if the equipment is available and approved for use.

This SOP covers electricity purchases, meter readings, outages, diesel where applicable, lubricants, grease, spare parts, filter cloths, packaging and cleaning supplies. Procurement and receiving also follow SOP 01.

### 2. Responsibilities

The Manager plans demand, arranges approved electricity and consumable purchases, supervises use, requests supplies and records applicable operating expenses. The Director retains palm kernel procurement responsibility, even where the Manager coordinates other supplies. The Manager does not enter actual credited, received, issued or returned quantities as a second measurement source.

The Storekeeper verifies units credited, takes meter readings, measures physical receipts, issues and returns, maintains source logs and reports unexplained differences directly to the Director. Operators use materials for approved work, collect them through the Storekeeper, report waste and return unused supplies. The Director approves purchases and corrections and reviews unusual consumption.

### 3. Electricity purchase and receipt

1. The Manager plans replenishment before prepaid units are exhausted and obtains the required Director approval.
2. The Storekeeper records the meter balance before crediting. The Manager or Director creates an Electricity purchase authorisation with the date, supplier and approved unit cost.
3. Keep the payment receipt. The Storekeeper verifies the actual kWh credited, allowing for electricity used between readings.
4. In Pending Weighing the Storekeeper enters the actual credited quantity in kWh and chooses Confirm Receipt. The app creates the purchase and stock receipt once.
5. Keep opening, credited and closing meter readings in the electricity log; report discrepancies. Do not re-enter the purchase as another inventory receipt or charge the same electricity purchase again in Expenses.

### 4. Daily electricity monitoring and outages

Electricity used (kWh) = opening meter balance + units credited − closing meter balance. Example: 300 + 200 − 180 = 320 kWh used.

The Storekeeper records the readings and calculated use in the electricity log. There is no dedicated Storekeeper meter-reading/consumption screen. The Director posts any required approved Electricity issue in the manual Inventory Report using these verified figures; the Manager does not supply a second quantity.

Switch off unused equipment and report unusual consumption. During an outage, stop and secure machines against unexpected restart. The Manager distinguishes a supply failure from a factory fault and arranges a qualified electrician where required. Follow SOP 02, Section 4.15, for unfinished production and SOP 05 for safe isolation. Do not allow poor prepaid-unit planning to cause avoidable stoppages.

### 5. Diesel, when applicable

Receive diesel through the authorised purchase and Storekeeper Confirm Receipt workflow. Measure and store it safely in suitable labelled containers away from heat and ignition sources. The Manager requests fuel for approved equipment; the Storekeeper measures and records the issue and remaining balance, and Operators refuel safely. Record generator operating hours only when a generator is actually in use.

Where daily consumption is measured, diesel used = opening quantity + quantity received − closing quantity. Include fuel remaining in equipment tanks so that transfers are not counted as consumption. Report leaks and shortages. For issues or returns without a dedicated Storekeeper app screen, keep the source log and obtain the approved Director manual posting; do not enter another purchase or duplicate stock receipt.

### 6. Lubricants, spare parts and other consumables

The Manager requests the correct material and intended use. The Storekeeper measures or counts the actual issue, records it in the consumables log and checks unused returns. Operators use it only for approved work, following machine maintenance instructions and SOP 05. No supplies may be taken without the Storekeeper’s knowledge.

The Director posts approved manual issues/returns for configured app items from the Storekeeper’s verified source log. Keep items not configured in the app in the supporting log. Monitor balances, report shortages and plan replenishment in time. The Manager completes applicable Expenses entries for separately charged services, avoiding duplicate charges.

### 7. Mandatory rules and non-compliance

The Storekeeper is the single physical measurement source. Verify credited electricity, record consumption and material issues, control all consumables and report waste, leaks and shortages. Preserve the distinction between purchases, usage and operating expenses. Theft, unauthorised use, falsified readings and deliberate concealment may lead to disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

## SOP 04 · Sales, Weighing and Product Dispatch

**SOP number:** SOMAD-SOP-004. **Sales arrangements and loading supervision:** Mill Manager. **All physical quantities:** Storekeeper. **Loading and pump operation:** Production Operators. **Prices, payment terms and release approval:** Director.

### 1. Purpose and scope

Find reliable buyers, obtain approved selling terms, dispatch the correct measured quantity, verify payment or approved credit, and keep linked sales and inventory records for PKO, PKC and sludge. This applies to customer collection and company-arranged delivery.

### 2. Responsibilities and sales arrangements

The Manager finds buyers, compares offers and reliability, negotiates proposed terms, checks transport/loading responsibilities, obtains Director approval and coordinates collection and Operators. The Storekeeper confirms stock availability and is the single source of actual dispatch weights. Operators are responsible for preparing products, lifting and positioning them for the Storekeeper’s weighing, operating assigned pumps and loading under supervision. The Director approves commercial terms, credit and product release and resolves serious disputes.

The Storekeeper does not negotiate prices, approve sales or collect customer payments. The Manager does not enter a second set of physical weights.

### 3. App authorisation and approved price

1. Director saves the approved product unit price and effective date on Prices. Refer an incorrect or unavailable price to the Director before authorising the sale.
2. Manager or Director selects the sale date, customer and PKO, PKC or Sludge in the Sales form. The app uses the applicable saved price; the Manager does not enter the dispatched quantity or a second price in this form.
3. Choose Authorise Sale to create a Pending Weighing task. This alone does not issue stock or prove dispatch, payment or permission to release goods.
4. Inform the Storekeeper of the approved collection and ask Operators to prepare suitable equipment and containers. Proposed customer quantities are planning information; the final actual quantity comes from the Storekeeper.

### 4. Physical weighing and loading

#### 4.1 PKO

1. The Storekeeper checks available stock. The Manager checks that the receiving tank/container is suitable and ready; the Operator checks the pump, valves and hoses.
2. The Storekeeper records the tank quantity before loading using the approved measurement method.
3. The Manager instructs the Operator to pump. The Storekeeper monitors the quantity and tells the Operator when to stop.
4. After the transfer stops and the reading settles, the Storekeeper takes the final measurement and calculates the actual net dispatch.
5. The Manager checks that the pump and valves are closed safely. Report leakage or spillage; do not conceal it in the customer’s quantity.

PKO dispatched = tank weight before loading − tank weight after loading. Use the actual measured amount, even if the order quantity differs.

#### 4.2 PKC

The Storekeeper confirms availability and weighs the PKC on the Director-approved basis. Deduct bag weight only when the Director instructs; do not make an automatic bag deduction. Apply the agreed container-weight instructions and record whether bags are included or excluded in the final measured quantity. Operators bring material to the weighing area and load the verified quantity under Manager supervision. No extra material is loaded after weighing without Storekeeper measurement and recording.

#### 4.3 Sludge

Operators collect sludge in suitable containers separate from PKO. The Storekeeper records newly recovered sludge through Receive Product → Sludge under production; this creates available stock once. When an approved sludge sale is ready, the Storekeeper weighs the net quantity dispatched under that sale authorisation. Do not count collection and sale as two receipts. No sludge is sold, given away or disposed of without Director approval.

### 5. Confirm payment and release

1. The Director confirms verified payment or approves credit terms. A transfer screenshot or alert alone is not sufficient proof.
2. The Storekeeper confirms the measured quantity and reviews the calculated sale total in Pending Weighing.
3. The Manager confirms the correct product is loaded and obtains Director permission for release. If the final amount depends on the weight, verify the final payment or approved credit before allowing the vehicle to leave.
4. The Storekeeper enters the actual quantity, vehicle number and scale ticket where available, then chooses Confirm Dispatch. This saves the sale and linked stock issue together.
5. The Manager allows physical release only under the approved terms. The app does not itself enforce the Director’s payment/release approval; staff must keep that supporting approval.

### 6. Sales records, invoices and receipts

The saved sale uses the Storekeeper’s actual quantity and approved saved unit price. The app calculates the total and creates its invoice identity. Do not submit a separate Manager quantity report or duplicate inventory issue.

Use the sale’s Details controls to view or print its invoice and statement. Director or Manager records verified customer payments through the sale payment controls and generates the corresponding receipt. A sales invoice is not a payment receipt. Keep payment approvals and evidence; the Storekeeper does not record payments. Do not record an approved credit sale as fully paid unless the payment has actually been received.

Record separately incurred delivery services in Expenses where applicable. Do not charge the same cost in two places. Storekeeper reviews remaining stock and reports differences; Director reviews linked records and Checks.

### 7. Shortages, complaints and corrections

Stop dispatch where necessary, keep the customer complaint and physical evidence, and have the Storekeeper recheck the measurement. Refer the decision on additional loading, correction, refund or other action to the Director. Use the appropriate Director correction controls; do not alter weights to hide shortages or add an unrelated inventory movement.

The Director cannot reduce a sale below recorded payments without resolving the payments. Payment receipts must not be silently discarded to delete a sale. Preserve supporting correction evidence in the approved company record rather than assuming the app provides a complete who-and-why audit log.

### 8. Mandatory rules and non-compliance

Use approved prices and commercial terms. The Storekeeper measures every dispatch and records it once. Confirm payment or approved credit and obtain the Director’s release approval. Do not remove, give away or sell goods without authorisation. Report actual quantities and discrepancies honestly. Unauthorised sales, undisclosed commissions, false measurements, false payment records and release without approval may lead to disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

## SOP 05 · Equipment Maintenance and Workplace Safety

**SOP number:** SOMAD-SOP-005. **Operations:** Mill Manager. **Lifting, unloading and loading:** Production Operators. **Physical measurements and supplies logs:** Storekeeper. **Approval and review:** Director.

### 1. Purpose

This SOP explains how workers must take care of factory machines and work safely.

The main goals are to:

- Prevent machine breakdowns and unnecessary repair costs.

- Keep machines in good working condition.

- Prevent injuries, electrical accidents and fires.

- Ensure machines are inspected and serviced regularly.

- Make sure faults and accidents are reported quickly.

### 2. Scope

This SOP applies to everyone working in the factory.

It covers the crusher, roaster, expeller, conveyors, filter press, pumps, electrical control panel and other factory equipment.

It also covers daily inspections, routine maintenance, repairs, emergency shutdowns, fire safety and personal protective equipment (PPE).

### 3. Staff Responsibilities

#### 3.1 Mill Manager

The Mill Manager must:

1. Ensure machines are inspected before use.

2. Plan and supervise regular servicing and maintenance.

3. Assign cleaning, inspection and lubrication duties to Operators.

4. Stop the use of faulty or unsafe equipment.

5. Report machine breakdowns and serious safety problems to the Director.

6. Arrange approved repairs by qualified technicians.

7. Keep records of maintenance, breakdowns and repairs.

#### 3.2 Production Operators

The Operators must:

1. Inspect their assigned machines before starting work.

2. Operate machines according to approved instructions.

3. Carry out basic cleaning and lubrication as instructed.

4. Report unusual noise, overheating, vibration, leakage or damage.

5. Keep machine guards and safety devices in place.

6. Wear the required protective equipment.

7. Stop work immediately when there is a serious danger.

#### 3.3 Storekeeper

The Storekeeper must:

1. Receive, verify and record spare parts, lubricants and maintenance materials.

2. Issue these items when requested and authorised.

3. Measure and keep the source record of quantities issued and unused quantities returned. For app-tracked consumables without a Storekeeper issue/return screen, send the verified record to the Director for an approved inventory entry; do not enter a second receipt for an already completed purchase.

4. Monitor available spare parts and maintenance supplies.

5. Report missing or damaged stock directly to the Director.

The Storekeeper is not responsible for repairing or operating factory machines.

#### 3.4 Director

The Director must:

1. Approve major repairs and equipment replacements.

2. Review serious breakdowns and safety incidents.

3. Approve purchases of required maintenance equipment and supplies.

4. Ensure serious safety problems are addressed before normal operations continue.

### 4. Maintenance And Safety Procedures

#### 4.1 Daily Machine Inspection

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Production Operators

Before production starts:

1. The Mill Manager assigns each Operator the machines to inspect.

2. The Operators check their machines for loose bolts, damaged parts, leaks and visible blockages.

3. The Operators check that protective guards and covers are properly fitted.

4. The Operators check belts, conveyors and other visible moving parts for damage while the machines are stopped.

5. The Operators check that oil outlets, pipes and connections are secure.

6. The Operators report any fault to the Mill Manager.

7. The Mill Manager checks reported faults and decides whether the machine is safe to operate.

No machine with a serious safety fault may be started until the problem has been corrected.

#### 4.2 Machine Lubrication

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operators

Materials Control: Storekeeper

1. The Mill Manager identifies which machines need lubrication, the correct lubricant and the required frequency.

2. The Mill Manager requests the lubricant from the Storekeeper.

3. The Storekeeper measures, issues and records the quantity in the consumables log. The Director posts any approved manual app movement from that record, as described in the workflow guide.

4. The assigned Operator lubricates the approved points following the machine instructions and required safety precautions.

5. The Mill Manager checks that the work has been completed correctly.

6. The Storekeeper records any unused lubricant returned.

##### Important rules

- Use only the correct lubricant for each machine.

- Do not overfill oil chambers or apply excessive grease.

- Do not lubricate exposed moving parts while machinery is running unless the equipment has a manufacturer-approved safe lubrication system.

- Do not allow lubricants to contaminate palm kernels, PKO or PKC.

#### 4.3 Regular Machine Servicing

Responsible: Mill Manager

Supporting: Production Operators

The Mill Manager must maintain a servicing schedule based on each machine’s manufacturer’s instructions.

##### Daily maintenance

- Inspect machines before use.

- Check for leaks, loose parts and unusual sounds.

- Check lubrication levels where required.

- Remove accessible dirt and material buildup after safe shutdown.

##### Weekly maintenance

- Check belts, chains and conveyor parts for wear or incorrect tension.

- Inspect bolts, bearings and visible machine connections.

- Check pumps, hoses and filter press parts for leaks or damage.

- Check the condition of roaster supports, drive parts and other accessible components.

##### Monthly maintenance

- Review machine condition and repeated faults.

- Check servicing requirements against actual operating hours.

- Arrange inspection of parts that require trained technicians.

- Check electrical connections and protective equipment through a qualified electrician where inspection is due.

The manufacturer’s servicing instructions take priority over these general intervals. Any work requiring special tools, electrical testing or machine dismantling must be assigned to a competent technician.

#### 4.4 Machine Breakdown

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Production Operators

When a machine develops a fault:

1. The Operator stops the affected machine when it is safe to do so.

2. The Operator informs the Mill Manager immediately.

3. The Mill Manager checks the reported problem without exposing anyone to moving or energised parts.

4. The Mill Manager decides whether the fault can be corrected safely by trained staff or requires a technician.

5. If a technician is required, the Mill Manager informs the Director and obtains the necessary repair approval.

6. The Mill Manager records the fault, time of breakdown, repair carried out and time production resumed.

7. The machine must be checked before being returned to normal use.

Operators must not attempt repairs beyond their training.

#### 4.5 Safe Shutdown Before Cleaning or Repair

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Authorised Operators or Technicians

Before anyone clears a blockage, removes a guard or works inside a machine:

1. The Mill Manager instructs the Operator to stop the machine.

2. The authorised person disconnects or isolates the correct power supply.

3. The equipment must be secured against accidental restart using the approved isolation and lockout procedure.

4. Any stored energy, including pressure, heat and moving parts that may continue turning, must be made safe.

5. The authorised person checks that the machine cannot start before work begins.

6. Only after these checks may cleaning or repair begin.

7. When work is complete, the Mill Manager checks that guards are restored, tools are removed and workers are clear before authorising restart.

Switching off a machine at its normal control button is not enough when someone must work inside it.

#### 4.6 Electrical Safety

Responsible: Mill Manager

Electrical Repairs: Qualified Electrician

1. The Mill Manager ensures the electrical panel is kept closed and protected from water, oil and dust.

2. All employees must report damaged cables, loose sockets, burning smells, electric shocks or unusual electrical behaviour immediately.

3. Only authorised workers may operate the normal machine controls.

4. Electrical repairs, wiring changes and testing must be carried out by a qualified electrician.

5. The Mill Manager must stop the use of any machine suspected of having a dangerous electrical fault.

6. The affected equipment must remain isolated until it has been inspected and declared safe.

##### Important rules

- Do not touch electrical equipment with wet hands.

- Do not wash electrical panels or motors with water.

- Do not bypass circuit breakers, overload protection or other safety devices.

- Do not repeatedly reset a tripping breaker without identifying the cause.

- Do not use damaged cables or exposed wiring.

#### 4.7 Roaster and Hot Equipment Safety

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

The roaster, hot kernels and expeller parts can cause serious burns.

1. The assigned Operator checks that the roaster is safe before heating begins.

2. The Mill Manager ensures the approved temperature and operating limits are followed.

3. Operators must keep hands, clothing and tools away from the rotating drum and hot surfaces.

4. Heat-resistant gloves and other suitable protection must be used when handling hot equipment or materials.

5. The Operator reports overheating, smoke, unusual smells or poor drum rotation immediately.

6. The Mill Manager stops heating and applies the approved emergency procedure if the equipment becomes unsafe.

7. Hot equipment must be allowed to cool safely before cleaning or repair, unless a specific safe procedure allows otherwise.

A loaded roaster must not be left unattended in a dangerous condition after a power failure.

#### 4.8 Filter Press and Pump Safety

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Assigned Operator

1. The Operator checks hoses, connections and filter plates before starting filtration.

2. The Mill Manager confirms the press is securely closed.

3. The Operator monitors pressure and leaks while the pump is running.

4. If pressure rises above the approved limit or a connection begins leaking dangerously, the Operator stops the pump and informs the Mill Manager.

5. Before opening the press, the Mill Manager ensures the pump is isolated and all internal pressure is released.

6. The Operators open and clean the press only after it is safe.

Never open a pressurised filter press or disconnect a hose while the system is under pressure.

#### 4.9 Fire Prevention and Emergency Response

Responsible: Mill Manager Applies To: All Employees

The factory must have suitable fire extinguishers and clear exits.

##### Fire prevention

1. The Mill Manager ensures that fire extinguishers are present, accessible and inspected as required.

2. All employees must keep exits and walkways clear.

3. Smoking and open flames are prohibited in production, storage and fuel -handling areas.

4. Oil spills and combustible waste must be cleaned up promptly.

5. Operators must report overheating motors, burning smells, smoke or dangerous fuel leaks immediately.

##### If a fire starts

1. Anyone who notices the fire must raise the alarm immediately.

2. If it is safe to do so, the main electricity mains to the mill should be put off.

3. All employees must leave through a safe exit.

4. Trained workers may attempt to put out a small fire using the correct extinguisher, and only where it is safe and there is a clear escape route.

5. Emergency services must be contacted immediately where required.

Never use water on an electrical fire or burning oil.

No employee should put themselves at risk to save machinery or products.

#### 4.10 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Responsible: Mill Manager Applies To: All Employees

Workers must use protective equipment suitable for their tasks. Approved protective wear will be communicated in due time.

Task| Required protection
General factory work| Safety footwear and suitable work clothing

Dusty kernel handling| Suitable dust mask or respirator, where required by the dust risk
Handling hot kernels or equipment| Suitable heat            -resistant gloves and protection
Machine operation| Appropriate eye and hearing protection where needed

Cleaning and chemical handling| Suitable gloves and eye protection
Heavy material handling| Safety footwear and appropriate handling equipment

Important: Loose clothing, jewellery and gloves that could catch in rotating machinery must not be worn near exposed moving parts.

The Mill Manager must ensure workers are trained to use their protective equipment correctly.

#### 4.11 Accidents and Injuries

Responsible: Mill Manager Applies To: All Employees

1. Any employee who witnesses an accident must raise the alarm and inform the Mill Manager immediately.

2. Dangerous machinery must be stopped if this can be done safely.

3. Emergency medical assistance must be called for serious injuries.

4. The Mill Manager informs the Director immediately.

5. The Mill Manager records what happened, when it happened, who was involved and the action taken.

6. The affected machine or work area must not be used again until it is safe.

#### 4.12 Maintenance Records

Responsible: Mill Manager Spare Parts Records: Storekeeper

1. The Mill Manager keeps a record of servicing, repairs, faults and machine stoppages.

2. Each maintenance entry must include the machine, date, work carried out and person responsible.

3. The Storekeeper measures and records spare parts, lubricants and other consumables issued for maintenance. Keep the maintenance and consumables logs outside the app where there is no matching screen; Director posts approved manual inventory movements for app-tracked items.

4. The Mill Manager reports major repairs and repeated faults to the Director.

5. Replaced parts that may be reused, returned or sold must be handed over to the Storekeeper for recording. Disposal requires authorisation.

### 5. Mandatory Safety Rules

These rules apply to everyone in the factory:

1. No employee may operate machinery without proper training and authorisation.

2. Never put hands or tools into moving machinery.

3. Never remove machine guards while the machine is operating.

4. Never clear a blockage without safely isolating the machine.

5. Do not open a filter press while it is under pressure.

6. Do not repair electrical equipment unless qualified and authorised.

7. Never bypass electrical or machine safety devices.

8. Do not operate a machine with a serious fault.

9. Report unusual noise, overheating, smoke, vibration or leakage immediately.

10. Wear the required protective equipment.

11. Keep fire exits and work areas clear.

12. Smoking and open flames are prohibited inside the mill.

13. All accidents and dangerous incidents must be reported immediately.

14. No employee may carry out unauthorised repairs or alterations to machines.

### 6. Non-Compliance

Any employee who deliberately ignores safety instructions, operates unsafe machinery, removes safety guards without authorisation, hides faults or carries out dangerous repairs may face disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

#### 3.1 Mill Manager

2. Ensure the factory remains organised during production.

3. Supervise full factory cleaning at the close of work.

#### 3.3 Storekeeper

#### 3.4 Director

### 4. Housekeeping Procedure

#### 4.1 General Tidying During Production

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Production Operators

Storage Area Support: Storekeeper

The factory must remain tidy throughout production. Workers must not wait until closing time to remove materials that could obstruct work or cause accidents.

##### Procedure

1. Operators must keep the areas around their assigned machines organised.

2. Tools, bags and other materials must be placed in their designated positions after use.

3. Spilled kernels must be collected promptly using clean tools.

4. Oil spills and leaks must be reported and attended to immediately.

5. Empty bags, shells and other unwanted materials must not be allowed to accumulate around machines.

6. Walkways and access to machines must remain clear.

7. The Storekeeper must assist with arranging bags, materials and other items in the storage area during busy production periods.

8. The Mill Manager must monitor the general condition of the factory and instruct workers to correct untidy or unsafe areas.

Important: Routine sweeping and full machine cleaning are reserved for the end of the working day. However, spills, obstructions and other hazards must be dealt with immediately during production.

No employee may clean inside or near exposed moving parts while a machine is running.

#### 4.2 Full Factory Cleaning at the Close of Work

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: All Production Operators

Full cleaning must be the last task carried out each working day, after production, product handling and other factory activities have been completed.

##### Procedure

1. The Mill Manager confirms that production and other activities have finished.

2. Operators shut down their assigned machines and ensure they are safe for cleaning.

3. The Mill Manager assigns cleaning duties to the Operators.

4. Operators sweep the production floor and collect loose kernels, shells, dust and other waste.

5. Operators clean the crusher, conveyor areas, roaster discharge area, expeller surroundings and filter press area as instructed.

6. Operators remove oil stains and clean slippery areas.

7. Operators clean the weighing and loading areas.

8. Operators arrange bags, tools and equipment in their designated places.

9. Operators collect rubbish and place it in the appropriate waste containers.

10. Operators clean and arrange the storage areas.

11. Operators return cleaning materials and tools to their proper locations.

12. The Mill Manager inspects the entire factory after cleaning.

13. Any area not properly cleaned must be corrected before staff leave.

No Operator may leave the factory at normal closing time without completing their assigned cleaning duties, unless excused by the Mill Manager.

Machine cleaning must follow the safety procedures in SOP 05.

#### 4.3 Storage Area Housekeeping

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Production Operators

Supported By: Storekeeper

Although cleaning the storage areas is the responsibility of the Production Operators, the Storekeeper must assist with tidying these areas during busy production work to allow the Operators to focus on production.

##### Palm kernel storage

1. Keep bags off wet floors using suitable pallets or raised supports.

2. Protect kernels from rainwater, dirt and moisture.

3. Keep the storage area free from standing water and pests.

4. Arrange bags safely without blocking access.

5. Keep empty bags and other materials properly arranged.

##### PKO storage

1. Keep the outside of the oil tank clean.

2. Clean oil spills around the tank promptly.

3. Keep tank covers, outlets and valves properly secured.

4. Do not allow dirt or water to enter the tank.

##### PKC storage

1. Keep PKC away from rain and standing water.

2. Avoid direct contact with dirty floors.

3. Remove spilled cake and keep the area tidy.

4. Report signs of mould, unusual heating or contamination.

#### 4.4 Factory Compound and General Facilities

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Production Operators

1. Keep the factory entrance, loading area and walkways tidy throughout the day.

2. Remove rubbish and unwanted materials from the compound during the final daily cleaning.

3. Keep drainage channels free from blockages.

4. Prevent standing water around machines and electrical equipment.

5. Keep toilets, handwashing areas and staff facilities clean.

6. Keep waste containers covered where necessary to discourage pests.

### 5. Waste Management

#### 5.1 Separation of Waste Materials

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Production Operators

Waste and recovered materials must be kept separate according to their type.

1. Kernel shells and foreign materials: Collect separately for approved reuse or disposal.

2. Spilled kernels: Keep separate until the Mill Manager checks whether they can be reused.

3. Oil spills: Collect and clean safely. Do not mix recovered dirty oil with finished PKO.

4. Sludge: Store separately from PKO and other waste.

5. Filter press residue: Collect in the designated containers.

6. Damaged bags and packaging: Keep in the appropriate waste area.

7. Used filter cloths: Collect separately after replacement.

8. Used lubricants and oily materials: Store in suitable, labelled containers.

9. General rubbish: Place in designated waste containers.

#### 5.2 Handling Oil Spills

Responsible: Mill Manager Work Done By: Production Operators

1. Stop the leak or oil transfer if it is safe to do so.

2. Inform the Mill Manager.

3. Keep workers away from the slippery area.

4. Use suitable absorbent materials or approved cleaning methods to remove the oil.

5. Place contaminated cleaning materials in the designated waste container.

6. Clean and dry the affected floor before normal work continues.

7. The Mill Manager records significant spills, while the Storekeeper verifies any measurable stock loss.

Oil collected from a dirty floor must not be mixed with finished PKO.

#### 5.3 Sludge and Filter Press Residue

Responsible: Mill Manager

Work Done By: Production Operators Stock Recording: Storekeeper

1. Operators collect sludge from the settling tank and filter press when instructed.

2. Sludge must be placed in suitable containers and kept separate from finished PKO.

3. The Storekeeper weighs sludge intended for sale and uses Receive Product → Sludge under the open daily production authorisation. This saves the production receipt and stock movement once; do not create a second inventory receipt.

4. The Mill Manager ensures the containers are kept in the designated storage area.

5. No sludge may be sold, removed or disposed of without the Director’s approval.

6. Sludge sales and dispatch must follow SOP 04.

#### 5.4 Waste Removal and Disposal

Responsible: Mill Manager Stock Verification: Storekeeper

Approval: Director

1. The Mill Manager identifies waste that needs to be removed.

2. The Storekeeper checks whether any of the materials are recorded as company stock or have value.

3. The Director approves removal or disposal where required.

4. Operators are responsible for lifting and loading the waste safely under the assigned instructions.

5. The Mill Manager ensures that waste is taken to an appropriate disposal or collection point.

6. The Storekeeper measures and records any company stock removed. A sale follows Authorise Sale → Pending Weighing → Confirm Dispatch. For approved disposal or another movement without a dedicated Storekeeper screen, Director posts the approved adjustment using the Storekeeper’s verified quantity. Do not also record a sale for the same disposal.

No employee may take waste, sludge, damaged parts or other company materials for personal use without authorisation.

### 6. Mandatory Housekeeping Rules

1. Full factory cleaning must be the last task of every working day.

2. All Production Operators must take part in the final daily cleaning.

3. General tidying must continue throughout production.

4. The Storekeeper must assist with keeping the storage areas tidy during busy production periods.

5. Spills and dangerous obstructions must be dealt with immediately.

6. Never clean inside moving machinery.

7. Do not pour oil, grease or used lubricants into drains.

8. Do not mix contaminated materials with palm kernels, PKO or PKC.

9. Keep sludge intended for sale separate from ordinary waste.

10. Do not remove or sell company waste materials without authorisation.

11. The Mill Manager must inspect the factory after cleaning and before closing.

### 7. Non-Compliance

Employees who deliberately refuse cleaning duties, leave their assigned areas dirty, contaminate company products, dispose of materials without approval, create unsafe conditions or remove company materials without permission may face disciplinary action, including termination of employment.
